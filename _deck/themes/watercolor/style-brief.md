# watercolor - illustration style brief

Soft watercolour illustration on white paper. Visible wash edges and gentle
pigment pooling, the way real watercolour settles - not a flat vector fill and
not an airbrushed gradient. Paper tooth should be faintly readable in the
lighter areas. Brushwork is confident and economical: a few deliberate strokes
rather than fine hatching or stippling.

Palette: muted teals and soft blue-greys for water, sky, distance and shadow;
warm sand, ochre and terracotta for buildings, wood and ground; deep ink-navy
only for the few darkest accents, used sparingly. Keep saturation low to
middling throughout. Natural colours stay natural - skin tones, timber, foliage
and stone are not pushed toward the palette.

Subject matter is calm and ordinary: mountain and valley landscapes, hot-spring
village rooftops, timber buildings, bridges, streams, trees, and people going
about unremarkable business. People are rendered simply, at middle distance,
individual but not portrait-detailed: a posture and a silhouette rather than a
face. No caricature, no manga styling, no heroic poses.

Composition leaves room. The subject occupies the middle of the frame with the
edges fading toward white, so the illustration can sit on a page beside text
without a hard boundary. Edges should dissolve into the paper rather than stop
at a line. No frame, no border, no vignette, no cast shadow under the whole
image.

Light is soft and directional, as on an overcast morning. One light source, no
dramatic contrast, no rim lighting, no lens effects, no bloom.

Keep it flat in the sense that matters: no 3D render look, no plastic sheen, no
emboss, no bevel, no glass. It should read as something a person painted.
